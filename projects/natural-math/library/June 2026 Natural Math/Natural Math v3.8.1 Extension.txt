**Natural Math v3.8.1 Extension**  
**Signal‑Based Coordination with Open Finite Energy Supply**

---

### Abstract

Natural Math v3.8.1 provides a discrete framework for growth driven by local energy gradients, pressure, and bifurcation. This extension replaces the growth‑oriented dynamics with a **coordination‑oriented** model in which a fixed number of nodes communicate through complementary signals, attract or repel one another, and form persistent bonds. The energy resource remains finite, but the system is made **open** by coupling it to an external, bounded energy reservoir – the system can draw energy from the outside, yet that outside source is not infinite. The result is an *open finite* system that self‑organises into stable clusters without requiring internal energy creation.

---

### 1. Core Principles Retained from Natural Math v3.8.1

- **Quadrance** \(Q(\mathbf{p}_1,\mathbf{p}_2) = \|\mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_2\|^2\) defines distance‑based geometry.
- **Energy** is a finite resource per node; initial energy \(E_0\) is given.
- **Local neighbourhoods** are bounded by a *sense_radius* \(R\).
- **Nodes** have unique identifiers, positions, and energy levels.

The original growth machinery (pressure accumulation, bifurcation, conflict resolution) is removed. The lattice is relaxed to a continuous 2D or 3D space.

---

### 2. Signal‑Based Encoder‑Message‑Decoder System

Each node carries a **signal type** \(s_i \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\):
- \(+1\) and \(-1\) are complementary polarities (attract and bond).
- \(0\) is neutral; when distressed, it repels other neutrals.

#### 2.1 Energy Deficit Function

A node’s distress is quantified by its energy deficit relative to full capacity:

\[
\delta_i = \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{E_0 - E_i}{E_0}\right) \tag{1}
\]

- \(\delta_i = 0\): full capacity, healthy.
- \(\delta_i = 1\): fully depleted.
- **Distress threshold**: \(\delta_i > 0.8\) triggers repulsive signalling.
- **Complementary threshold**: \(\delta_i \le 0.6\) allows attraction between complementary types.

#### 2.2 Signal Encoding

Node \(i\) broadcasts a signal whose strength depends on its state:

\[
\sigma_i = 
\begin{cases}
+\alpha_{\text{comp}}, & \text{if } s_i \neq 0,\; \delta_i \le 0.6 \text{ and a complement is nearby}\\
-\beta_{\text{distress}}, & \text{if } \delta_i > 0.8\\
-\gamma_{\text{default}}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \tag{2}
\]

Typical values: \(\alpha_{\text{comp}} = 60\), \(\beta_{\text{distress}} = 100\), \(\gamma_{\text{default}} = 10\).

#### 2.3 Signal Transmission (Spatial Physics)

A signal sent by node \(i\) arrives at node \(j\) (if within \(R\)) after a delay and with attenuation:

\[
\text{delay}_{i\to j} = \frac{\|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j\|}{v_s}, \qquad
\text{attenuation}_{i\to j} = \exp(- \lambda \cdot \|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j\|) \tag{3}
\]

where \(v_s\) is signal speed and \(\lambda\) is decay constant. The received strength at \(j\) is \(\sigma_i \cdot \text{attenuation}_{i\to j}\).

Parameters: \(v_s = 2.0\), \(\lambda = 0.1\), \(R = 10.0\).

#### 2.4 Weighted Decoding and Decision Gradient

Node \(j\) accumulates incoming messages using inverse‑square weighting and complementarity logic. The effective force from node \(i\) is

\[
F_{i\to j} = \frac{\sigma_i}{Q(\mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{p}_j) + \varepsilon}
             \times 
             \begin{cases}
               3, & \text{if } s_i = -s_j \text{ (complementary)}\\
               1, & \text{otherwise}
             \end{cases} \tag{4}
\]

where \(\varepsilon \ll 1\) prevents division by zero.  
The resulting **social force vector** is

\[
\mathbf{F}_j = \sum_{i \neq j} F_{i\to j}\, \frac{\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j}{\|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j\|} \tag{5}
\]

The movement gradient direction for node \(j\) is the normalised net force (or zero if \(|\mathbf{F}_j| = 0\)):

\[
\mathbf{g}_j = \frac{\mathbf{F}_j}{\|\mathbf{F}_j\|} \quad (\|\mathbf{F}_j\| > 0) \tag{6}
\]

No random fallback is used.

---

### 3. Bonding Mechanics

When two complementary nodes (\(s_i = -s_j\)) come within a small quadrance threshold (\(0 < Q(\mathbf{p}_i,\mathbf{p}_j) \le Q_{\text{bond}}\)) and both have free bond slots, they form a **persistent bond**:

- **Maximum bonds per node**: \(\text{max\_bonds} = 3\).
- **Bond threshold**: \(Q_{\text{bond}} = 4\) (Euclidean distance ≈ 2).

Upon bonding:
1. **Energy equalisation**: both nodes set their energies to the average  
   \[
   E_i' = E_j' = \frac{E_i + E_j}{2} \tag{7}
   \]
2. **Positional lock**: node \(j\) is snapped to the position of node \(i\) (or vice versa).  
   \[
   \mathbf{p}_j \leftarrow \mathbf{p}_i \tag{8}
   \]
3. **Bond registration**: each node records the partner’s ID.

Bonded pairs move together as a single unit thereafter, preserving their bond unless broken by extreme forces (not modelled here).

---

### 4. Energy Dynamics and Open Finite Energy Supply

#### 4.1 Internal Energy Costs

Every active step costs a fixed amount of energy:

\[
\Delta E_{\text{step}} = \eta \tag{9}
\]

with \(\eta = 0.5\). A node cannot move if \(E_i < \eta\).

#### 4.2 Open Finite Energy Reservoir

To make the system **open** yet **finite**, we introduce an external energy source of total capacity \(E_{\text{res}}^{\max}\). The reservoir is not infinite; once depleted, no further energy can be drawn.

Nodes can extract energy from the reservoir when they are within a designated *feeding zone* or when certain conditions are met (e.g., proximity to a source location). The extraction rate per node is bounded:

\[
\frac{dE_i}{dt}\bigg|_{\text{ext}} = 
\begin{cases}
\kappa, & \text{if } E_{\text{res}} > 0 \text{ and node is allowed to feed}\\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \tag{10}
\]

where \(\kappa\) is a constant uptake rate, and the global reservoir depletes accordingly:

\[
\frac{dE_{\text{res}}}{dt} = -\sum_{\text{feeding nodes}} \kappa \qquad (E_{\text{res}} \ge 0) \tag{11}
\]

Initial conditions: \(E_{\text{res}}(0) = E_{\text{res}}^{\max}\).  

This extension guarantees that **energy does not need to be created internally**; the system can acquire it from outside, but the outside source is finite – reflecting the real‑world constraint “everything is available, but there’s only so much you can get out of it.”

#### 4.3 Conservation and Limits

Total energy in the system (nodes + reservoir) is strictly non‑increasing:

\[
\sum_i E_i(t) + E_{\text{res}}(t) \le \sum_i E_i(0) + E_{\text{res}}^{\max} \tag{12}
\]

Energy is never created, only transferred or spent on movement.

---

### 5. Emergent “Stick” Behaviour

The interplay of gradient attraction, deficit‑driven repulsion, bonding, and saturation gives rise to **persistent clusters** (the “stick”):

1. Complementary healthy nodes attract and move toward one another.
2. When close enough, they bond, equalise energy, and lock positions.
3. Distressed nodes repel, preventing overcrowding and maintaining cluster stability.
4. Bonded clusters of 2‑4 nodes act as stable units, resistant to dispersal.

No external scaffolding is needed; the clusters emerge from the local signal logic.

---

### 6. Comparison with Core Natural Math v3.8.1

| Feature | Core (v3.8.1) | This Extension |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| Primary drive | Energy gradient → pressure → bifurcation | Signal complementarity → attraction/repulsion → bonding |
| Node count | Variable (growth) | Fixed |
| Energy source | Closed, internal | Open finite (external reservoir) |
| Distance weighting | \(1/d\) | \(1/d^2\) with complementarity boost |
| Bonding | Absent | Up to 3 bonds per node, positional lock |
| Geometry | 3D integer lattice | Continuous 2D/3D space |

---

### 7. Recommendations for Use

- Begin with the default parameters given above, then tune \(\alpha_{\text{comp}}\), \(\beta_{\text{distress}}\), and \(\kappa\) for desired cluster sizes.
- Monitor energy conservation (Eq. 12) and locality.
- Adapt oracles for the fixed‑node regime, e.g., cluster count, bond network topology.
- The model can represent cell adhesion, robotic docking, or logistics hub formation.
