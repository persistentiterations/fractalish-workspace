# The Fractalish Rift-Ribbon Thesis

## A Cross-Scale Theory of Preferential Transport Topology

**Status:** Exploratory / Non-Canonical  
**Umbrella:** Fractalish  
**Document type:** Living research paper and update log  
**Current version date:** 2026-08-02

## Working premise

Across physical scales, matter, energy, momentum, and information repeatedly organize into seams, sheets, ribbons, filaments, channels, faults, fronts, conduits, and branching networks.

The Fractalish Rift-Ribbon Thesis (FRRT) proposes that at least some of these structures may belong to a common transport family: preferential pathways generated by gradients, constraints, field alignment, rotation, accumulated stress, nonlinear feedback, and inherited history.

The thesis does **not** presently claim that one hidden force directly connects every ribbon-like structure, that all visual similarity implies common causation, or that every candidate structure is mathematically fractal.

---

## Core thesis

Matter and energy do not move evenly through nature. Under pressure, rotation, gradients, field alignment, accumulated history, and changing boundary conditions, systems repeatedly form rifts, ribbons, channels, sheets, and branching pathways that make some transfers easier than nearby alternatives.

The central question is:

> Are recurring ribbon-like and branching forms merely similar-looking outcomes produced by unrelated physics, or do they reveal a reusable architecture governing how movement organizes itself across scale?

## Dimensional Expression Principle

A recurring pattern should not be defined only by the shape visible in a two-dimensional image. Its apparent geometry may be a lower-dimensional projection, section, boundary, shadow, or trace of a higher-dimensional organizing structure.

### Dimensional ladder

- **Node:** concentration, source, sink, junction, or event.
- **Trace:** line-like evidence that movement occurred or became concentrated.
- **Ribbon or sheet:** a surface carrying flow, separating regimes, or defining a favorable boundary.
- **Corridor volume:** a region with thickness, internal gradients, and multiple possible routes.
- **Living corridor:** the structure across time as it forms, moves, folds, branches, merges, weakens, and closes.

### Fractalish Dimensional-Ribbon Hypothesis

Some recurring line networks are lower-dimensional observations of ribbon-like, sheet-like, tubular, membrane-like, or volumetric transport structures. The recurring pattern may not fundamentally be a picture. It may be a process in which local differences become boundaries, boundaries extend into paths, paths branch into networks, and networks organize later movement.

## Working vocabulary

### Rift
A location where a system separates, weakens, reconnects, changes regime, or permits transfer that was previously difficult.

### Ribbon
An elongated structure that carries, concentrates, organizes, or constrains movement.

### Rift-ribbon
A coupled structure in which an opening, boundary, weakness, or transition also becomes a preferred path for movement.

### Easy corridor
A path requiring less internally supplied effort than nearby alternatives. This does not imply zero resistance or free energy.

### Transport skeleton
The hidden geometry organizing where movement is likely to occur.

### Corridor field
The full changing environment of favorable and unfavorable pathways.

### Pattern inheritance
The process by which prior structure or flow changes the system and biases what forms or moves through it next.

### Corridor memory
Persistent physical or field change left by earlier transport, increasing the probability of related future transport.

### Handoff zone
A region where movement transfers from one corridor type, layer, or scale to another.

### Nested corridor
A smaller corridor existing within, beside, or because of a larger one.

### Ribbon node
A junction where ribbons meet, divide, exchange material or energy, or change scale.

### Ribbon basin
A surrounding region whose organization tends to guide movement toward a corridor or node.

### Cosmic boundary condition
An astronomical or space-environment influence that changes the probability, timing, location, or strength of a corridor without presuming an unknown force.

Identifying corridor formation and handoff conditions can enable lower-effort movement through natural and engineered environments.

## Anomaly-Marker Program

### Purpose

Known models, sensor systems, and observational programs already record departures from expected motion, timing, acceleration, drag, energy use, path shape, or arrival conditions. FRRT should treat these departures as **markers for investigation**, not immediate evidence of a ribbon.

The working principle is:

> An outlier is not the explanation. It is a location in the data where the assumed transport geometry may be incomplete.

### Candidate anomaly classes

- unexplained or poorly explained acceleration;
- unexpected deceleration or drag reduction;
- anomalous transit time;
- path deviation without an adequate modeled cause;
- sudden changes in velocity, heading, or energy use;
- unusual clustering of trajectories;
- repeated low-cost passage through the same region;
- apparent motion inconsistent with a flat or static model;
- short-lived openings or closures in transport;
- mismatches between predicted and observed arrival;
- unexplained synchronization across distant systems;
- unexpected persistence, handoff, branching, or merger of flow structures;
- sensor disagreements that follow a coherent spatial or temporal pattern;
- “unknown,” “unmodeled,” “residual,” or “other” classifications in existing datasets.

### Marker discipline

An anomaly becomes an FRRT candidate only after:

1. instrument error and data corruption are considered;
2. known local causes are modeled;
3. sampling and selection effects are assessed;
4. the event is placed in spatial and temporal context;
5. comparable controls are identified;
6. a corridor interpretation makes a distinct, testable prediction.

### Speakable term: Ribbon marker

A **ribbon marker** is an observed deviation, residual, outlier, or unexplained transition that may indicate a hidden transport boundary or preferential pathway.

### Speakable term: Corridor residual

A **corridor residual** is the part of observed motion or transfer left unexplained after the best available conventional model has been applied.

### Speakable term: Outlier constellation

An **outlier constellation** is a recurring group of anomalies that align in space, time, direction, or system state and may therefore be more informative than any single event.

## FRRT Manifold Discrimination Assay

The paper *Sky sphere representation in language models* provides a useful methodological example for FRRT. Its authors report that several large language models represent night-sky proximity on a decodable spherical feature manifold rather than a flat coordinate chart. The signal becomes prominent under proximity-focused prompts, and the authors explicitly test competing flat and three-dimensional explanations.

### Lessons adopted for FRRT

#### Projection Rival Test
Whenever a candidate ribbon is identified from a flat image, test whether the two-dimensional geometry is sufficient or whether a curved or higher-dimensional structure explains the relationships better.

#### Relational Geometry Recovery
Infer hidden geometry from neighborhood, ordering, separation, transition, and co-movement relationships rather than requiring direct coordinates for every point.

#### Question-Conditioned Visibility Principle
A hidden structure may remain invisible when observations are organized around the wrong relationship. Corridor-focused questions should emphasize adjacency, passage, separation, opening, closure, branching, merger, and handoff.

#### Irreducible Corridor Hypothesis
Some rift-ribbons may not be recoverable as a simple sum of independent measurements because their function resides in the curved relationship among those measurements.

#### Radial Meaning Test
Do not assume that depth, centrality, thickness, brightness, or radius in a reconstructed representation corresponds to literal physical distance. Test what it actually encodes.

### Assay sequence

1. Choose a candidate domain and relational question.
2. Gather observations across contexts and times.
3. Extract a transparent low-dimensional representation.
4. Fit competing geometries: flat map, line, tree, sphere, cylinder, ribbon, sheet, tube, or network manifold.
5. Validate on held-out objects, regions, or events.
6. Test the unique dimensions of rival explanations.
7. Record uncertainty and negative results.

## Texture-Blind Ribbon Test

Render candidate structures from different domains using a common visual grammar while removing labels, scale bars, familiar colors, and material cues.

Compare:

- branching-angle distribution;
- width-to-length ratio;
- curvature and torsion;
- node degree and junction topology;
- thickness distribution;
- nestedness and scale hierarchy;
- boundary sharpness;
- split-and-merge behavior;
- persistence through time;
- flow concentration or conductance.

A visually indistinguishable rendering is suggestive. Shared geometric, dynamic, and functional measurements are required for a stronger claim.

## Evidence ladder

- **E0 — Visual resemblance:** structures look similar after scale and texture cues are removed.
- **E1 — Geometric correspondence:** topology, branching, curvature, thickness, or nestedness converge.
- **E2 — Functional correspondence:** structures perform comparable roles in transport, separation, concentration, or handoff.
- **E3 — Generative correspondence:** comparable abstract constraints produce the structures in models or experiments.
- **E4 — Predictive correspondence:** a cross-domain model predicts formation, movement, or collapse better than domain-only baselines.
- **E5 — Causal intervention:** changing an identified driver reliably opens, redirects, strengthens, or closes the corridor.

## Research guardrails

- Do not call a pattern fractal solely because it branches or looks self-similar.
- Do not infer shared causation from shared appearance.
- Do not treat unknown cosmic influence as the default explanation when known local physics is sufficient.
- Do not merge scales without specifying carrier, force, timescale, and coupling mechanism.
- Do not call a route an easy corridor unless it reduces a defined cost relative to matched alternatives.
- Require out-of-sample prediction before claiming that planetary or cosmic variables add explanatory value.
- Preserve negative findings and failed correspondences.
- Treat anomalies as markers, not conclusions.
- Separate instrument anomaly, model residual, and physical anomaly.
- Preserve the original observation before interpretation.

## Foundational Orientation: Beyond the Accumulation Paradigm

### Working conviction

Reality may not be assembled primarily from isolated objects carrying properties. It may be organized through recurring relations, boundaries, flows, inheritances, accumulations, releases, and transitions, while the objects we catalogue are temporary local expressions of that deeper grammar.

This does not invalidate conventional physics. It changes what is catalogued first.

The Fractalish program therefore treats substrate-independent organization as a primary object of study. Atoms, organisms, planetary systems, and cosmic structures may differ radically in material, force, scale, and timescale while still exhibiting recurring organizational operations.

### The accumulation problem

The accumulation paradigm is the tendency to explain systems mainly by what they contain, how much they contain, and how those contents add together. That approach is powerful, but incomplete when the decisive behavior lies in how accumulation is constrained, redistributed, vented, inherited, converted, or prevented from becoming destructive.

FRRT will investigate whether recurring natural structures encode laws or regularities governing accumulation without relying on explicit symbolic instructions, centralized control, or a separately identifiable command signal.

Candidate organizational questions include:

- What permits accumulation?
- What limits it?
- What changes accessibility as accumulation grows?
- What converts accumulation into flow, branching, storage, release, or collapse?
- What allows a system to preserve capacity without unlimited concentration?
- What records the history of prior accumulation?
- What causes a basin, boundary, or corridor to form around accumulated matter, energy, stress, charge, momentum, or information?
- What determines whether accumulation becomes productive structure, persistent memory, congestion, instability, rupture, or dispersal?

### Substrate-independent inventory

The research objective is not merely to collect repeated shapes. It is to build a language describing recurring organizational roles before silo-specific terms divide them apart.

The first inventory should include at least:

- accumulation;
- dispersion;
- concentration;
- restriction;
- release;
- handoff;
- inheritance;
- boundary formation;
- corridor formation;
- basin formation;
- branching;
- merging;
- cycling;
- saturation;
- rupture;
- recovery;
- persistence;
- rebalancing.

Each term must eventually be defined operationally, not metaphorically, and tested across domains without assuming identical physics.

### Provisional Fractalish proposition

> Repeated patterns across scale may be visible records of how systems regulate accumulation, preserve continuity, and redistribute constrained flow.

Under this proposition, ribbons, basins, branches, filaments, membranes, faults, channels, shells, and nodes are not merely forms. They may be receipts of prior constraint and transfer.

### Ethical orientation

The work is not organized around accumulation of ownership, status, or acclaim. Its purpose is corrective: to improve the inventory, expose where existing categories conceal recurrent organization, and develop tools that may reduce harmful concentration and improve continuity, access, distribution, and recovery.

This ethical orientation is a project commitment, not scientific evidence. Scientific claims remain subject to measurement, comparison, prediction, and falsification.

### Current foundation statement

> Fractalish seeks a substrate-independent grammar of organization. FRRT studies how that grammar may become visible as recurring boundaries, ribbons, basins, branches, corridors, and transitions. The accumulation program asks why systems permit, constrain, transform, distribute, inherit, or terminate concentration—and whether those recurring responses can be measured across scale without confusing shared organization with identical mechanism.

## 2026-08-02 — Contemporaneous Multi-Sensor Snapshot Program

### Working proposition

FRRT should begin with synchronized observations rather than retrospective visual matching alone. The aim is to assemble common UTC windows in which atmospheric, oceanic, seismic, geomagnetic, heliophysical, tidal, geodetic, and orbital measurements overlap closely enough that proposed relationships cannot be dismissed merely as comparisons between unrelated dates or locations.

### Primary data institutions

NASA, NOAA, USGS/NSF SAGE, ESA, ECMWF/Copernicus, Copernicus Marine, INTERMAGNET, and related national observatory networks provide the most practical starting concentration of openly accessible, calibrated, time-stamped measurements. No single institution spans every domain; the program must federate their archives.

### Existing adjacent correlations

Established work already links portions of the stack: ocean waves generate seismic microseisms; wind and atmospheric pressure deform the ground and contaminate or inform seismic records; ocean, atmosphere, ice, and hydrologic loading stress the crust; Earth and lunar tides deform the solid Earth and can weakly modulate seismicity under some conditions; lunar tides have also been detected in the plasmasphere; ocean-current products already combine satellite altimetry, wind stress, gravimetry, and in-situ observations.

A direct, global, preregistered comparison of jet-stream topology, seismic residuals, ocean-current structure, tides, geomagnetic state, heliophysical forcing, and relativistic/geodetic corrections as one FRRT corridor assay has not yet been identified.

### Snapshot design

Each snapshot should preserve:

- one common UTC interval;
- explicit spatial footprints and coordinate transformations;
- native cadence and uncertainty for every instrument;
- raw observations separately from reanalysis and modeled products;
- known forcing variables and conventional model predictions;
- residuals after those predictions;
- lagged windows rather than same-time correlation alone;
- negative-control locations and shuffled dates;
- provenance, calibration state, missingness, and processing version.

### Initial synchronized stack

1. Upper-atmosphere winds and pressure fields from ERA5 or equivalent reanalysis.
2. Ocean surface currents, sea level, waves, tides, and wind stress from NOAA and Copernicus Marine.
3. Continuous seismic waveforms and station metadata from NSF SAGE/EarthScope and national networks.
4. Geomagnetic observations from INTERMAGNET.
5. Solar-wind, interplanetary magnetic-field, energetic-particle, and magnetospheric measurements from NASA SPDF/CDAWeb and ESA space-weather services.
6. GNSS, gravity, Earth-orientation, and solid-Earth tide products for coordinate and loading controls.
7. Orbital drag or trajectory residuals where openly available.

### Interpretation rule

The project is not initially attempting to prove an unknown energy. It is testing whether a common, higher-dimensional transport description explains synchronized residual structure better than the accepted domain-specific models and known coupling pathways alone.

## 2026-08-02 — Observational Convergence and Organizational-State Forecasting

### Observational Convergence Opportunity

Humanity has accumulated detailed measurements across nearly every accessible physical domain, but those measurements were collected under different vocabularies, coordinate systems, scales, timescales, and institutional purposes.

Contemporary sensing, computation, and machine reasoning make it newly practical to recatalogue those observations around shared organizational behavior rather than disciplinary origin.

The opportunity does not arise because earlier researchers lacked intelligence or care. Many likely studied individual portions of the same ground in depth, using the best instruments and mathematics available to them. The new condition is convergence:

- dense and continuous sensing;
- simultaneous observations from many viewpoints;
- decades of historical archives;
- drone, satellite, buoy, station, and swarm measurements;
- automated geometry extraction;
- multimodal comparison across incompatible data forms;
- machine-assisted translation between disciplinary vocabularies;
- enough compute to preserve native resolution rather than reducing every system to a few averages;
- parallel generation and adversarial testing of rival explanations.

The research problem has therefore shifted. The central limitation is no longer only insufficient measurement. It is the lack of a shared organizational inventory capable of coordinating what has already been measured.

### Automated Geometry Extraction

FRRT should develop methods that extract structure before assigning disciplinary meaning.

Candidate outputs include:

- centerlines and flow axes;
- boundaries, shells, sheets, and membranes;
- ribbon width, thickness, curvature, and torsion;
- branching and junction graphs;
- nested scales and internal substructures;
- split, merger, reconnection, and handoff events;
- direction and rate of transport;
- boundary sharpness and permeability;
- persistence, recurrence, drift, and collapse;
- basin structure and changing accessibility;
- residual geometry not explained by the accepted domain model.

The system should be tested both with domain labels available and with labels withheld. If a classifier can identify the source domain only from texture, color, scale, or familiar context, that does not establish a shared law. More meaningful evidence would arise if the same abstract operators describe formation and transition after those cues are removed.

### Cross-Domain Laws of Organization

A **domain law** describes the local material, force, and mechanism producing an event.

A **cross-domain organizational law** describes a recurring transformation performed by systems despite differences in substrate and local physics.

A provisional organizational sequence is:

> distinction or gradient → concentration of flow → boundary formation → preferential passage → pathway modification → inheritance → branching, merger, saturation, rupture, redistribution, or recovery

This sequence must not be presumed universal. It is a candidate grammar to be operationalized and tested.

The purpose is not to force geology, biology, plasma physics, atmospheric science, cosmology, and computation into one equation. It is to determine whether they share measurable operators at a level more abstract than material identity and more rigorous than visual analogy.

### Vocabulary Inversion

Scientific catalogues usually begin with the local object and its measurable properties. FRRT proposes an additional first pass:

> Before asking what a structure is made of, ask what organizational role it is performing.

A jet stream, fault, plasma sheet, vascular branch, river delta, magma conduit, activation manifold, and cosmic filament may remain physically distinct while still participating in recurring organizational roles such as:

- separating regimes;
- concentrating transfer;
- retaining accumulation;
- changing accessibility;
- creating a handoff;
- preserving pathway memory;
- opening under alignment;
- closing under saturation or loss of support;
- redirecting later formation.

**Vocabulary inversion** is the correction of naming structures primarily by substrate and then mistaking those names for complete descriptions of organizational kind.

### Organizational Re-Cataloguing

FRRT should maintain a **Pre-Domain Organizational Inventory** in which candidate structures are first described by relations and transformations:

- What distinction creates the structure?
- What does it separate?
- What does it carry?
- What does it retain, attract, exclude, or release?
- How does it alter nearby accessibility?
- Does passage modify the path?
- Does prior history make reuse more likely?
- How does it grow, fold, branch, merge, saturate, rupture, recover, or close?
- What larger structure contains it?
- What smaller structures form within it?
- What trace or receipt remains after it disappears?

Material, scale, force, coordinate system, and disciplinary labels are added after this organizational record is preserved.

### Organizational-State Forecasting

FRRT should attempt to forecast changes in organizational state before limiting itself to named event prediction.

Possible forecast statements include:

- a boundary is sharpening;
- a basin is becoming more retentive;
- accumulation is approaching a switching threshold;
- a weak path is becoming accessible;
- a corridor is gaining continuity;
- two pathways are approaching a handoff;
- a ribbon is beginning to branch or reconnect;
- a transport structure is losing support;
- saturation is approaching rupture;
- redistribution or recovery is becoming likely.

This leads to the working proposition:

> Forecast the emergence, strengthening, handoff, saturation, rupture, closure, and recovery of transport structures before forecasting only the named events they may produce.

A structure may become measurable before its familiar consequence is recognizable within a silo-specific vocabulary.

### Adversarial Analysis Architecture

The same computational power that makes cross-domain discovery possible can also manufacture persuasive false patterns. FRRT therefore requires a deliberately adversarial workflow.

For each proposed correspondence:

1. A discovery system identifies a candidate shared structure.
2. A domain system searches for accepted local explanations.
3. A measurement system tests sensor artifacts, calibration failures, missingness, and preprocessing effects.
4. A geometry system tests projection, rescaling, registration, smoothing, and coordinate artifacts.
5. A temporal system tests lag flexibility, periodicity, multiple-comparison effects, and shuffled-time controls.
6. A prediction system evaluates held-out regions, instruments, and future windows.
7. A falsification system attempts to construct a simpler rival explanation.
8. A receipt system records why the candidate survived, weakened, or failed.

The objective is not to preserve the largest number of beautiful correspondences. It is to preserve the smaller set that remains after ordinary explanations and methodological artifacts have been exhausted.

### Compute and Institutional Freedom

Machine systems do not arrive without assumptions, and they can reproduce institutional blind spots embedded in data and training. Their advantage here is narrower and practical:

- they can retain more simultaneous context;
- translate between specialized vocabularies;
- compare large numbers of geometries and lag structures;
- preserve contradictory hypotheses in parallel;
- inspect records that no individual could read in a lifetime;
- repeat analyses consistently;
- expose the exact transformations used to create a result.

FRRT should use this capability to loosen disciplinary framing without abandoning domain expertise, measurement discipline, or known physics.

### Current forecasting proposition

> Synchronized, multi-domain observations may permit forecasting of organizational transitions that remain invisible when each field is interpreted only through its inherited vocabulary.

## 2026-08-02 — Processor-Scale Energy Transients as an FRRT Test Domain

### Why this domain matters

Advanced classical and quantum processors offer a unusually instrumented micro-scale environment in which external and internally generated energy events can be observed through their effects on computation.

This domain is useful to FRRT because it combines:

- known carriers such as ionizing particles, phonons, quasiparticles, charge, heat, and mechanical vibration;
- precisely engineered geometry;
- densely timestamped error records;
- controlled interventions;
- repeatable beam testing;
- direct functional consequences in logic and qubit state;
- opportunities to compare local events, correlated bursts, pathways, boundaries, and mitigation structures.

The processor is therefore not merely an analogy for a rift-ribbon system. It may serve as a compact experimental substrate in which transport geometry, accumulation, thresholds, branching, propagation, and failure can be measured more directly than in many natural systems.

### Working distinction

The relevant established fields already include radiation effects in electronics, single-event effects, soft-error reliability, superconducting-qubit decoherence, quasiparticle poisoning, phonon transport, quantum error correction, thermal management, and reliability engineering.

FRRT does not replace those fields. Its contribution would be to ask a different organizational question:

> Can diverse processor errors be recatalogued as traces of transient transport structures rather than only as isolated component failures?

### Candidate FRRT objects

#### Energy transient

A localized or propagating transfer event capable of changing accessibility, state stability, or error probability within a processor.

#### Transient corridor

A temporary route through which deposited energy, charge, phonons, quasiparticles, heat, or stress propagates more effectively than through nearby alternatives.

#### Error ribbon

A spatially and temporally coherent sequence of faults that may mark the path, boundary, or wake of an energy transient.

#### Threshold crossing

The moment when accumulated or concentrated disturbance exceeds the local capacity of a logic element, memory cell, resonator, qubit, or correction system to preserve state.

#### Correlated burst

A cluster of errors whose shared timing and geometry indicate a common event or propagating disturbance rather than independent local noise.

#### Mitigation topology

An engineered arrangement of shielding, trapping, isolation, redundancy, geometry, cooling, or correction that redirects, absorbs, fragments, or terminates a transient.

### Initial organizational sequence

> incident or internal disturbance → local deposition → carrier conversion → propagation through substrate and interfaces → concentration or dispersion → threshold crossing → correlated error field → trapping, dissipation, correction, or persistent damage

This sequence should be compared across classical and quantum devices without assuming that the physical carriers or failure mechanisms are identical.

### Research opportunities

1. Reconstruct transient pathways from synchronized error timing across a processor.
2. Compare observed error geometry with chip layout, material boundaries, thermal gradients, workload state, and shielding.
3. Test whether correlated errors form line-like, ribbon-like, sheet-like, radial, branching, or basin-like structures.
4. Identify whether repeated disturbances inherit preferred routes from fabrication geometry, prior damage, local heating, or operating history.
5. Compare natural radiation events with controlled beam, mechanical, thermal, and electrical interventions.
6. Treat mitigation designs as deliberate corridor engineering: trapping, rerouting, segmentation, boundary creation, and controlled dissipation.
7. Test whether organizational-state forecasts can identify an approaching burst before formal logic or qubit failure is registered.

### Important factual boundary

Current research supports the existence of single-event effects in classical electronics and correlated radiation-, vibration-, quasiparticle-, and phonon-related errors in superconducting processors. It does not yet support describing all such effects as one newly discovered energy field or as direct evidence for universal FRRT corridors.

The FRRT opportunity is methodological: use a highly measured substrate to determine whether a shared transport-topology vocabulary adds predictive or engineering value beyond the established device-specific models.

### Candidate pilot study

**Processor Transient Topology Atlas**

For each event, preserve:

- source or trigger class;
- timestamp and duration;
- deposited or inferred energy;
- spatial error footprint;
- chip layer and material;
- device operating state and workload;
- temperature and mechanical state;
- propagation delay between affected elements;
- correlation with particle detectors or controlled interventions;
- accepted local explanation;
- unexplained residual;
- reconstructed path geometry;
- threshold and recovery behavior;
- mitigation response;
- repeatability under matched conditions.

The first test should determine whether event classes that are presently separated by disciplinary vocabulary share measurable organizational signatures after carrier-specific physics is retained.

## 2026-08-02 — Processor Transient Early-Warning Program

### Immediate objective

Establish whether a transport-topology representation of processor disturbances adds any reproducible predictive value over conventional event, correlation, and device-physics baselines.

The first claim must remain narrow:

> Using only information available before a registered failure, can an evolving geometric representation predict the next affected component, region, or correlated burst better than established baselines?

### Minimum viable proof

A funding-grade result does not require a universal theory. It requires:

- a clearly defined prediction target;
- a timestamped dataset with event ordering;
- one or more accepted baselines;
- strict separation of training and held-out evaluation;
- measurable improvement;
- reproducible code and data lineage;
- interpretable receipts showing which evolving structure supported each prediction.

### Recommended first target

**Next-region prediction in correlated superconducting-qubit error bursts.**

Given the first portion of an observed burst, predict:

1. whether the event will expand beyond its initial footprint;
2. which qubit or processor region is affected next;
3. the likely spatial extent;
4. the remaining burst duration or recovery interval.

This target directly tests the FRRT proposition that a fault belongs to an evolving transport structure rather than existing only as an isolated component error.

### Team data-mining brief

For every candidate dataset, record:

- canonical paper title and DOI;
- repository and durable identifier;
- license and reuse restrictions;
- whether data are raw, processed, or figure-only;
- spatial layout of qubits, gates, memory cells, or sensors;
- native timestamp or cycle ordering;
- event labels and trigger source;
- detector coincidence data;
- temperature, workload, vibration, shielding, and operating context;
- intervention or control condition;
- whether individual events can be reconstructed;
- whether the next state can be predicted without future leakage;
- baseline models used by the authors;
- code availability;
- missing variables;
- contact author and institution.

### Data acceptance levels

- **Level A:** raw event-level, timestamped, spatially resolved data with controls.
- **Level B:** event-level data with partial geometry or context.
- **Level C:** processed arrays sufficient to reconstruct sequences.
- **Level D:** figure-level data useful only for replication or method prototyping.
- **Level E:** paper-only evidence; bibliography and outreach candidate.

The first predictive assay should use Level A or B data. Level C and D may support pipeline development but cannot carry the primary claim.

### Baseline suite

At minimum, compare FRRT features against:

1. event frequency and independent-error baseline;
2. nearest-neighbor spatial correlation;
3. distance-weighted hazard model;
4. autoregressive event-history model;
5. standard graph or surface-code decoder where applicable;
6. conventional machine-learning model using the same pre-event variables;
7. ablation model without geometric or topology features.

The topology layer earns credit only for improvement beyond the strongest fair baseline using identical information.

### Initial FRRT feature set

- active-event centroid;
- propagation direction;
- expansion rate;
- distance from inferred origin;
- local and global event density;
- front curvature;
- anisotropy;
- branching count;
- connected-component evolution;
- boundary contact;
- chip-layer or material-interface contact;
- persistence;
- quiescent gaps;
- handoff between neighborhoods;
- estimated basin or trap behavior;
- uncertainty in each reconstructed feature.

### Evaluation rules

- Split by complete event, experimental run, and preferably device.
- Never split adjacent samples from the same burst across training and test sets.
- Lock the prediction horizon before evaluation.
- Report class imbalance and calibration.
- Use shuffled-time, shuffled-layout, and rotated-layout controls.
- Test whether performance survives removal of event-source labels.
- Preserve a simple physical baseline.
- Report failures and negative transfer across devices.

### Funding threshold

The initial threshold should not be stated as a fixed percentage before inspecting event rates and operational costs. The result becomes interesting when it is:

- statistically credible;
- reproduced across held-out runs;
- operationally useful at an acceptable false-alarm rate;
- attributable to topology features rather than data leakage;
- sufficiently interpretable to guide mitigation or experiment design.
